A bedroom door needs an extra nudge to close. The latch almost meets the plate, but the top edge brushes the frame. Before anyone forces it, the door needs a calm look at where it is rubbing.

A sticking door needs a diagnosis before a plane. Find the rub, tighten what is loose, and separate a simple latch adjustment from moisture or frame movement.

Quick answer

Find where the door rubs, tighten hinge screws, and check latch alignment before making any cut. Do not force or plane a door until you know whether loose hinges, humidity, a swollen edge or a moving frame is the cause.

Methods that actually help — try them in this order

Start with the lowest-risk, reversible method. Move on only if it does not explain or improve the sticking door.

Method 1

Locate the rub

Best for: a door that catches

  1. Close it slowly, look for shiny rub marks or use a thin paper strip around the gap to identify the tight point.
  2. Work in good light and take a photo before changing anything.
  3. Change one variable at a time so you can tell what helped.
  4. Stop if the issue worsens or exposes a safety concern.
Method 2

Tighten hinges first

Best for: a sagging latch side

  1. Support the door and tighten loose hinge screws by hand; replace stripped fixings only with the correct repair.
  2. Work in good light and take a photo before changing anything.
  3. Change one variable at a time so you can tell what helped.
  4. Stop if the issue worsens or exposes a safety concern.
Method 3

Check latch alignment

Best for: the door reaches the frame but will not latch

  1. Mark where the latch meets the plate and make only a small, deliberate adjustment if the frame is sound.
  2. Work in good light and take a photo before changing anything.
  3. Change one variable at a time so you can tell what helped.
  4. Stop if the issue worsens or exposes a safety concern.
Method 4

Reduce moisture and retest

Best for: seasonal swelling

  1. Ventilate the room, address nearby damp, and let the timber stabilise before removing material.
  2. Work in good light and take a photo before changing anything.
  3. Change one variable at a time so you can tell what helped.
  4. Stop if the issue worsens or exposes a safety concern.
Method 5

Book a carpenter for controlled trimming

Best for: persistent rubbing or frame movement

  1. A professional can assess hinges, frame and door leaf, then plane or repair only the confirmed contact point.
  2. Work in good light and take a photo before changing anything.
  3. Change one variable at a time so you can tell what helped.
  4. Stop if the issue worsens or exposes a safety concern.

Common scenarios

It sticks only in humid weather

Check ventilation and nearby damp; avoid permanent trimming until it stabilises.

The latch misses but the gaps look even

The strike plate may need a minor alignment check.

The door suddenly will not close

Look for a loose hinge, swollen edge, obstruction or visible frame movement.

Please don’t do this

These shortcuts can make a small problem harder to diagnose or more expensive to repair.

Don’t 1

Don’t 2

How to keep it from returning

  • Tighten hinge screws during routine cleaning.
  • Keep thresholds and frames dry.
  • Lubricate hinges lightly with a suitable product.
  • Avoid repeated slamming.
  • Address leaks near timber early.
Practical tip

Keep a simple note of when and where you first see the sticking door. That pattern makes a professional visit faster and more useful.
