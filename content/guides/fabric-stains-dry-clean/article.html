A favourite outfit catches a bright splash just before you leave. The instinct is to rub, rinse, or reach for the strongest product nearby. For a delicate garment, the calm first move is usually the one that saves it.

Quick answer

Blot fresh stains, keep heat away, and read the care label. Silk, wool, embellished clothes, oil stains, and unknown marks should go to a dry cleaner with the stain identified.

Why stains become permanent

Heat can set protein stains, rubbing pushes oil and pigment deeper, and an untested cleaner can remove colour before it removes the stain. The fibre matters as much as the spill: a washable shirt and a lined, embellished garment do not get the same treatment.

  • Protein: milk, egg, blood, and sweat can set with heat.
  • Oil: cooking oil and makeup spread with scrubbing.
  • Dye and ink: water or solvent can make them travel.
  • Delicate fibres: wool, silk, velvet, suede, leather, and embellishment need specialist handling.

Try these in order

These steps preserve the garment and give a cleaner useful information. They are not a substitute for treatment on a dry-clean-only item.

Method 1

Read the label

Best for: every special garment

Read the fibre and care label. “Dry clean only,” silk, wool, leather, suede, lining, beads, or embroidery are reasons to stop at blotting.

Method 2

Blot and lift solids

Best for: fresh food spill

Lift solids with a spoon or blunt edge. Then blot with a clean white cloth, working from the outside inward. Do not scrub.

Method 3

Keep it cool and unheated

Best for: protein or unknown stain

Keep heat away. Do not iron, tumble-dry, steam, or use hot water until the stain is gone.

Method 4

Tell the cleaner what happened

Best for: professional treatment

Tell the cleaner the stain, fabric, when it happened, and every product already used. A photo taken before blotting can help.

Method 5

Store it breathable

Best for: before drop-off

Keep the garment dry and loose in a breathable bag. Never seal a damp item in plastic.

Cleaner’s shortcut

For a special item, preserve the evidence: blot, photograph, label the stain, and stop. A cleaner can remove a stain; they cannot undo colour loss from a home experiment.

Common scenarios

Fresh food spill on a special garment

Lift solids, blot from the outside in, and check the label before water or heat.

Oil or makeup on embellished fabric

Do not scrub. Identify the stain for the cleaner and keep the garment cool and dry.

Yellow mark after a previous home attempt

Tell the cleaner every product already used. Older oxidation is harder to remove but still worth professional assessment.

What not to do

Don’t 1

Rub an oil stain

Rubbing drives grease into the fibres and enlarges the mark.

Don’t 2

Iron over a stain

Heat can set food, drink, protein, and unknown stains.

Don’t 3

Bleach an unknown or embellished fabric

Bleach can strip colour, weaken fibres, and damage embroidery.

Don’t 4

Seal a wet garment in plastic

Trapped damp causes odour, mould, and colour transfer.

Keep it from returning

Use a napkin at meals, keep cosmetics and oil away from special fabrics, and store cleaned garments dry in breathable covers.

When to book a professional

Book dry cleaning for silk, wool, suede, leather, embroidery, oil stains, old yellowing, and garments with a dry-clean-only label.

FAQs

Should I use water on every fresh stain?

No. Check the care label first. Water can spread dye, alter delicate fibres, and set some stains when combined with heat.

What should I tell a dry cleaner?

Name the stain, the fabric, when it happened, and what you have already applied.

Can a dry cleaner remove an old stain?

Sometimes, but older stains and previous treatment make removal less certain. Take it in promptly.

Which garments should go straight to dry cleaning?

Dry-clean-only clothing, silk, wool, suede, leather, embellished pieces, valuable garments, and anything with oil, ink, dye, or an unknown stain.
