Quick answer

Stop forcing the handle, find where the sash rubs, dry the area if damp, and ease only the marked high spot after the wood has dried. Loose hinges, a shifted frame, painted-shut edges, or a sash that still binds after careful easing needs a carpenter.

Stop here

Do not lever the sash with a screwdriver, plane a wet window, or ignore rot, soft timber, or a handle that turns without moving the latch.

What usually causes this

The visible symptom is useful, but the small detail around it usually tells the real story.

  • Timber swelling after rain, snowmelt damp, or a humid spell.
  • Layers of paint gluing the sash edge to the frame.
  • Loose hinge screws letting the sash drop and catch on the sill.

Try these in order

Begin with the reversible checks. The goal is a clean fix, not winning an argument with a stubborn household thing.

Method 1

Stop forcing the handle

Best for: any jammed sash

Extra leverage snaps handles and splits rails — open slowly and note exactly where it binds.

Method 2

Mark the rub and check hinges

Best for: a sticking edge or dropped sash

Look for shiny scrape marks, tighten hinge screws if firm wood remains, and pencil the high spot.

Method 3

Dry before you cut

Best for: a damp swollen edge

If the timber feels wet or the room just had rain entry, let it dry; planing wet wood often leaves a summer gap later.

Method 4

Ease only the marked high spot

Best for: minor seasonal swell after drying

A carpenter can shave a thin marked edge and reseal it — DIY planing of the whole sash often makes winter draughts worse.

Method 5

Book a carpenter

Best for: painted shut, cracked rail, or shifted frame

A pro can free painted edges, realign hinges, repair rot, and reseal so the window works through Kashmir wet and dry seasons.

Kashmir home note

Wooden windows in Kashmir take rain, snowmelt damp, and dry heated winters in the same year. A tight monsoon fit can free up in January — forcing a swollen sash often creates the draught you will hate next winter.

What not to do

These shortcuts usually cost more than they save.

Don't 1

Lever the sash with a screwdriver

Splits the rail

Prying between sash and frame bruises timber and can crack glass putty or the rail itself.

Don't 2

Plane wet swollen timber

Summer draught gap

Cutting wood while damp removes material you will miss when the sash shrinks in dry winter heat.

Don't 3

Force a painted-shut edge

Tears paint and wood

Paint that has glued the sash needs careful cutting free — shoulder force usually damages both faces.

Keep it from returning

Fix the small trigger, keep the area clean and dry where relevant, and deal with repeat symptoms before they become a replacement job.
