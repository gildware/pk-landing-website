In an older flat, the kitchen tube flickers whenever the geyser clicks on. The family swaps bulbs twice before anyone asks whether the fitting or circuit is talking.

Quick answer

Tighten or replace the bulb or tube, try another lamp on the same point, and note whether flicker matches appliance use. Persistent flicker, buzzing, heat, or one-room-only issues need an electrician.

Quick diagnosis map

Start with the symptom, not the most dramatic fix. A small check now can save a cabinet, appliance, or Sunday afternoon.

What you noticeLikely reasonFirst move
Light flickers, dims, or pulsesLoose bulb, failing driver, loose contact, or circuit loadSwitch off, tighten the bulb, or swap in a known-good lamp.
Problem worsens after useAn underlying fault or hidden damageStop adding load; inspect safely.
Heat, burning smell, leak, or sudden painSafety issue, not a DIY puzzleSwitch off / isolate / get help.
Stop here

Do not wiggle live fittings, bypass a holder, or ignore flicker that comes with a burning smell.

What usually causes this

The visible symptom is useful, but the small detail around it usually tells the real story.

  • Loose or incompatible bulb or LED driver.
  • Loose holder contact or old starter gear.
  • Heavy appliance starting on the same circuit.

Try these in order

Begin with the reversible checks. The goal is a clean fix, not winning an argument with a stubborn household thing.

Method 1

Reseat or replace the bulb

Best for: one flickering point

Switch off, tighten the bulb, or swap in a known-good lamp.

Method 2

Try another fitting

Best for: isolating bulb vs point

Move the bulb to a different holder to see whether the flicker travels.

Method 3

Listen and feel

Best for: buzzing or heat

Warm switches, buzzing holders, or scorch marks are not bulb problems.

Method 4

Note the pattern

Best for: load-related flicker

Write down whether it happens when the geyser, heater, or motor starts.

Method 5

Book an electrician

Best for: persistent or spreading flicker

Loose neutrals, damaged wiring, and bad holders need proper testing.

A pro's shortcut

Take one clear photo before taking anything apart. It helps a professional see the pattern and saves guesswork.

Kashmir home note

Winter load from heaters and geysers can make marginal circuits show symptoms that look like a bad bulb. Check the simple things first, but do not ignore heat or smell.

What not to do

These shortcuts usually cost more than they save.

Don't 1

Wiggle live fittings

Shock and short-circuit risk

Touching a live holder or twisting wires while power is on can cause a shock or spark. Switch off at the breaker before any check.

Don't 2

Bypass a holder with tape or wire

Creates a fire hazard

Taping wires or bypassing a holder leaves bare connections that can overheat. A proper holder or electrician fix is the safe route.

Don't 3

Ignore flicker with a burning smell

Often means overheating wiring

A burning smell with flicker usually points to arcing or a hot connection, not a bad bulb. Turn off the circuit and book an electrician.

Keep it from returning

Fix the small trigger, keep the area clean and dry where relevant, and deal with repeat symptoms before they become a replacement job.

When to book a professional

Book an electrician for heat, buzzing, scorch marks, whole-room flicker, or flicker that continues with a new bulb.

FAQs

What is the first check for lights flickering?

Switch off, tighten the bulb, or swap in a known-good lamp.

Can I keep using it?

Only if there is no safety risk or spreading damage. Stop when the symptom worsens.

Why did this start suddenly?

A change in load, moisture, wear, cleaning product, or season often exposes a weak point.

Will a temporary fix last?

It can buy time, but repeat symptoms need the cause corrected.

When should I book help?

Book an electrician for heat, buzzing, scorch marks, whole-room flicker, or flicker that continues with a new bulb.
