What usually causes this

The visible symptom is useful, but the small detail around it usually tells the real story.

  • Humidity swelling particleboard near the sink.
  • Runners knocked out of alignment during install.
  • Loose hinge screws letting doors hang crooked.

Try these in order

Begin with the reversible checks. The goal is a clean fix, not winning an argument with a stubborn household thing.

Method 1

Mark what rubs

Best for: any sticking door or drawer

Open slowly and note exactly where contact happens.

Method 2

Check hinges and runners

Best for: misaligned fronts

Look for bent runners, loose screws, and doors sitting uneven.

Method 3

Inspect wet zones

Best for: swelling near sink

Look for puffing laminate or a board that feels thicker at the edge.

Method 4

Tighten and adjust

Best for: minor misalignment

Use the correct screwdriver; adjust runner height in small steps.

Method 5

Call a carpenter

Best for: warped panels or repeated failure

A pro can re-square carcasses, replace runners, or re-edge swollen boards.

What not to do

These shortcuts usually cost more than they save.

Don't 1

Force soft-close drawers

Can bend runners and chip laminate

Forcing a stuck drawer stresses the runner and front edge; swelling needs adjustment, not muscle.

Don't 2

Drill new holes in finished fronts

Ruins the panel and rarely fixes alignment

Random holes in laminate fronts are hard to hide and may miss the real hinge or runner fault.

Don't 3

Ignore swelling near the sink

Board damage spreads with moisture

Puffing laminate at the sink edge usually means water is getting into the core; it will not flatten on its own.

Keep it from returning

Fix the small trigger, keep the area clean and dry where relevant, and deal with repeat symptoms before they become a replacement job.

When to book a professional

Book a carpenter for warped carcass panels, delaminating edges, repeated runner failure, or doors that will not align after basic adjustment.

Can I keep using it?

Only if there is no safety risk or spreading damage. Stop when the symptom worsens.

Why did this start suddenly?

A change in load, moisture, wear, cleaning product, or season often exposes a weak point.

Will a temporary fix last?

It can buy time, but repeat symptoms need the cause corrected.
