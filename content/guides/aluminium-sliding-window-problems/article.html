A sliding window starts scraping just as rain comes sideways. It is tempting to pull harder; usually the track is asking for cleaning, not a wrestling match.

Aluminium sliders should move on rollers, drain rain through small weep holes and seal when shut. Grit, blocked drainage and worn hardware interrupt all three. Start with the parts you can see; a tilted panel, cracked glass or loose frame is professional work.

Quick answer

Vacuum the track, clear visible drain holes gently, inspect seals and then use a window-safe dry silicone lubricant sparingly. Never force a jammed panel. Cracked glass, a loose frame, failed lock or continuing leak needs an aluminium-and-glass professional.

What normally goes wrong

  • Track grit: dust and grit turn rollers into sliders.
  • Blocked weep holes: rainwater cannot leave the frame.
  • Worn rollers: the panel drags, drops or rocks.
  • Flattened seals: wind-driven rain or drafts pass the closure.
  • Frame movement: alignment problems need more than lubrication.

Methods that actually help — try them in order

Method 1

Vacuum and brush the track

Best for: a dragging panel

Open the panel only as far as it moves safely. Vacuum loose grit, use a soft brush in corners and wipe with a barely damp cloth. Avoid flooding the channel.

Method 2

Find weep holes

Best for: rainwater in the frame

Find the small slots or holes on the outer side of the bottom frame. Clear loose dirt with a soft brush; do not drill, enlarge or push wire into hidden channels.

Method 3

Inspect rollers, seals and the close

Best for: draught or leak

With the window closed, look for flattened rubber, a gap at a corner, wobble or a panel that sits low. Note it; do not prise glass or remove the sash to investigate.

Method 4

Use dry silicone sparingly

Best for: clean but sticky tracks

Only after cleaning, use a small amount of dry silicone product made for window tracks. Wipe away excess and slide the panel slowly. Thick oils collect dust and can block drains.

Method 5

Have rollers adjusted

Best for: persistent scraping

A professional can adjust or replace rollers and align the panel without stressing glass. Tell them whether the trouble is dragging, leakage, a draft or a failed lock.

Leaks, drafts and locks

Water appears only during rain

Clear weep holes first, then check whether water is entering from above, a damaged exterior seal or a crack. A sill puddle is not proof that the track itself is the entry point.

The panel moves but will not lock

Do not slam it. A dropped roller or frame shift can stop the latch meeting its keeper; book adjustment.

Panel drags every winter

Cold can stiffen seals and thicken residue in the track. Clean first, then use a suitable dry silicone sparingly — not heavy grease.

Pro shortcut

Photograph the lower track, weep holes and closed-panel gaps before a visit. Those three images often explain whether the job is cleaning, hardware or sealing.

Please don’t do this

Don’t 1

Force a stuck panel

Excess force can bend the frame, damage rollers or break glass.

Don’t 2

Use heavy oil or grease

It catches dust, thickens in cold weather and creates another blockage.

Don’t 3

Keep using cracked glass or a loose sash

Secure the area and arrange repair; this is not a track-cleaning task.

Don’t 4

Drill extra drain holes

Random holes weaken the frame and can create new leak paths. Use only the manufacturer’s drainage route.

Keep it from returning

When to book help

Book an aluminium-and-glass professional for cracked glass, a loose or dropped panel, frame movement, persistent leakage after the drains are clear, failed locks, damaged seals or rollers that need adjustment.

FAQs

What lubricant is safe for aluminium window tracks?

Use a window-safe dry silicone product sparingly after cleaning. Avoid thick oils and grease.

Why is rainwater on my sill?

Blocked weep holes are common, but water can also enter from a failed seal, crack or the wall above the window.

Can I lift the panel out myself?

Not unless you know the system and can safely handle the glass. Large panels are heavy and can be damaged or fall.

Should I drill more drain holes?

No. Use only the manufacturer’s drainage route; drilling can weaken the frame and make leaks worse.

When is a stuck window urgent?

When it is an emergency exit, has cracked glass, is loose, cannot lock, or lets significant water into the room.
